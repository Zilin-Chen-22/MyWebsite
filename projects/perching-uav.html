Flexible Perching System for a Miniature UAV

A graduation thesis integrating a lightweight aerial platform, deformable adhesion, visual servoing, and control for autonomous approach, attachment, and release.

Giving a small drone somewhere to rest.

The project addresses a fundamental limit of miniature UAVs: flight time. By attaching to a surface, the vehicle can stop producing continuous thrust and remain in place with minimal energy.

Lightweight system design

I designed and integrated the airframe base, support structure, upward-looking camera, high-voltage circuit, and flexible attachment actuator around strict mass and center-of-gravity constraints. The platform weighs approximately 48.4 g before the final VSERF material is added, leaving margin beneath the actuator's measured load limit.

Flexible attachment mechanism

The attachment module uses voltage-switchable electrorheological fluid (VSERF), which changes its mechanical state under an electric field to form a compliant adhesive interface. Optical isolation allows the low-voltage flight controller to safely trigger the high-voltage module, supporting attachment, sustained perching, and controlled release.

Perception and control

An AprilTag pipeline performs calibration, distortion correction, pose estimation, and online relative-position measurement. I analyzed the weaker depth observability of monocular projective geometry, then combined a slower visual outer loop with cascaded attitude and angular-rate PID loops. Stability was studied with Hurwitz and Lyapunov methods.

Simulation-to-hardware validation

A MuJoCo environment reproduced the full task from takeoff and visual alignment through approach and attachment, including partial tag loss near the target. Physical experiments then demonstrated autonomous approach, stable attachment for 60 seconds without continuous lift, and controlled release after the adhesive voltage was removed.
